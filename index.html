RESEARCH VISION

From matrix dynamics to biological organization.

My research program, Interactions Bioengineering, seeks to identify how interacting physical, structural, and biochemical cues in the extracellular matrix regulate cellular and multicellular states. Rather than treating matrix properties as independent variables, I investigate how they evolve together through cell-driven remodeling.

Building on fundamental studies of hydrogel mechanics, collagen degradation, and cell–matrix interactions, I aim to translate these mechanisms into predictive biomaterials and physiologically relevant multicellular models.

RESEARCH THEMES

Four connected directions.

Four interconnected questions define the scope of my emerging research program, from the molecular consequences of ECM remodeling to cell behavior, multicellular architecture, and disease-relevant transitions.

01

ECM–Immune Interfaces

How does the environment around a cell change the way the immune system sees it?

Cancer cells can escape detection by the immune system. I study how this immune evasion is linked to matrix degradation, with the goal of understanding the mechanisms that connect the extracellular environment to immune recognition.

Why it mattersBetter cancer–immune models and new strategies to overcome immune evasion.
02

Multicellular Organization

How does the surrounding matrix guide cells to organize into complex structures?

Cells integrate cues such as adhesion, mechanics, and degradation to migrate and organize into multicellular structures. I study how these interactions can be controlled to guide organoid organization and function.

Why it mattersBuilding more controllable organoids that better reproduce the organization and function of real tissues.
03

Enzyme-Programmed Materials

Can enzyme–ECM interactions be used to predict and program dynamic material states?

Enzymes can simultaneously alter matrix structure, mechanics, and confinement. I study how these coupled changes can be quantified and modeled to predict and program how biomaterials evolve over time.

Why it mattersDesigning adaptive biomaterials with predictable and programmable properties.
04

ECM & Disease Progression

How does a changing tissue environment accompany disease progression?

I connect engineered models with patient tissues to understand how matrix remodeling and cellular states change together during disease.

Why it mattersMore physiologically relevant models for studying disease transitions.

EMERGING RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Measure, map, predict, reconstruct.

Future capabilities that will connect engineered microenvironments with quantitative measurements of cell and tissue states.

01

Mapping biological state spaces

Integrating spatial transcriptomics, spatial proteomics, tissue architecture, and extracellular matrix features to define how molecular and structural states are organized across tissues.

02

AI-guided state-space discovery

Developing data-driven and AI-enabled approaches to identify relationships among matrix properties, molecular states, cellular phenotypes, and tissue organization.

03

Visualizing dynamic matrix remodeling

Developing imaging-based approaches to track matrix displacements over time and quantify how local degradation produces time-dependent mechanical relaxation.

04

Reconstructing biological states

Using engineered extracellular matrices to reconstruct predicted microenvironmental states and test how they regulate cellular and multicellular behavior.

ABOUT

Badri Narayanan Narasimhan, Ph.D.

I am a Postdoctoral Scholar in Bioengineering at the University of California, San Diego, developing an independent research program focused on interactions between extracellular matrices, cells, and tissues.

My research investigates how extracellular matrix remodeling regulates cellular organization, mechanosensation, and disease progression. I integrate engineered biomaterials, mechanobiology, quantitative modeling, and spatial profiling to understand how matrix remodeling, mechanics, and adhesion shape cellular states.

My research trajectory spans dissipative hydrogel design, collagen microarchitecture and degradation, degradation-induced stress relaxation, and the reconstruction of disease-relevant microenvironments.
